# Opening-night analysis — October 7, 2025

## Scores, shots on goal, and shot attempts

**The projected winner matched all three games, but the model overpredicted scoring and shots on goal across the night.** Winner selection was the strongest observed result; shot volume, opportunity allocation, and conversion showed substantial errors. Three retrospective games are not enough to establish general predictive accuracy.

## Where the predictions were strong

### Correct ordering of the teams

Florida, Pittsburgh, and Colorado all had higher expected goals than their opponents and all won. However, the projected advantages were small:

| Winner | Projected goal advantage | Actual goal advantage |
|---|---:|---:|
| FLA | 0.149 | 1 |
| PIT | 0.100 | 3 |
| COL | 0.340 | 3 |

This supports the limited statement that the ordering was right that night. It does not demonstrate accurate win probabilities, confident winner selection, or an ability to predict the realized margins. Expected margins need not equal single-game outcomes.

### Some useful team and player estimates

- Florida's 3.304 goals versus 3 actual was the closest scoring estimate without an empty-net complication. Its 34.144 SOG versus 37 was also relatively close.
- Pittsburgh's 31.649 SOG versus 31 was the closest full-team shot estimate. Its apparently close 3.158 goals versus 3 needs the empty-net qualification below.
- NYR's player TOI MAE was 1.145 minutes, the lowest of the six teams. Braden Schneider was projected for 18:10 and played 18:06.
- Nathan MacKinnon's baseline attempts were 5.936 versus 6 actual; Jason Dickinson's were 1.996 versus 2. These are examples of close estimates, not evidence that all downstream conversion was correct.

Paired Sep2026 SAT/TOI inputs covered 103 of 108 skaters. All player SAT counts reconciled with stored game summaries, and shot-fact goals/SOG reconciled with boxscores. Those checks support the integrity of the actuals used here, rather than the accuracy of the model.

## Where the predictions struggled

### Too much scoring, especially for NYR and LAK

| Game | Combined goals predicted | Actual | Error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CHI–FLA | 6.459 | 5 | +1.459 |
| PIT–NYR | 6.216 | 3 | +3.216 |
| COL–LAK | 6.570 | 5 | +1.570 |

NYR was projected for 3.058 goals and scored none; LAK was projected for 3.115 and scored one. Those two outcomes account for 5.173 of the night's 6.245 net excess goals. A shutout alone does not invalidate a positive expected-goals estimate, but repeated scoring overprediction is a pattern to investigate over a larger sample.

All six expected scores clustered between 3.058 and 3.455, while actual scores ranged from zero to four. Expected values naturally vary less than realized scores; this is a reason to inspect predictive distributions, not to force expected scores to reproduce single-game extremes.

### Chicago and Colorado had large SOG overpredictions

Chicago was projected for 31.208 SOG and recorded 19: **+12.208**. Colorado was projected for 36.352 and recorded 23: **+13.352**. Together they account for 25.560 of the night's 33.090 net excess SOG.

MacKinnon illustrates why attempts and conversion need separate evaluation: his baseline SAT estimate was close, but his SOG estimate was 4.339 versus 2. Mika Zibanejad shows a different failure: 3.932 baseline attempts versus 12, and 2.386 SOG versus 7, despite only a 1:09 TOI underestimate. TOI alone cannot explain every attempt or conversion miss.

Justin Brazeau was projected for 2.243 attempts, 1.608 SOG, and 0.154 goals; he recorded 8, 6, and 2. Martin Necas had 0.324 baseline xGF and scored twice. These large individual outcomes require distributional evaluation across many games; they do not by themselves establish that the expected values should have been two goals.

## SAT coverage: two different questions

The historical profiles exclude unknown shot type and empty-net events. The reconciliations contain **119 unknown-type attempts** and **two empty-net attempts**, leaving **234 modeling-eligible attempts** out of 355 full-game attempts.

| Team | Predicted SAT | Full-game actual SAT | Eligible actual SAT | Error versus eligible subset |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CHI | 45.285 | 50 | 32 | +13.285 |
| FLA | 50.988 | 69 | 50 | +0.988 |
| PIT | 46.845 | 61 | 39 | +7.845 |
| NYR | 44.886 | 60 | 38 | +6.886 |
| COL | 53.799 | 49 | 33 | +20.799 |
| LAK | 47.121 | 66 | 42 | +5.121 |

The diagnosis changes with the target: **66.076 attempts below full-game actuals, but 54.924 above the eligible subset**. Every team exceeds that subset, especially Colorado and Chicago. Therefore, neither “raise all SAT predictions” nor “the SAT model is simply too low” is justified by the full-game comparison.

Predicted aggregate SOG/SAT is approximately 66.8%, versus 45.1% using all actual attempts. Because their attempt populations differ, that gap alone does not isolate an accuracy/finishing defect. The independently observed SOG overprediction remains real. Coverage and conversion must be reconciled before selecting a correction.

## Defense: useful visibility, incomplete validation

Defensive projections were available for 103 of 108 skaters. Missing entries were Ben Kindel, Harrison Brunicke, Noah Laba, Gabriel Landeskog, and Gavin Brindley. These players had offensive fallbacks but contributed nothing to the defensive composition. That can make a partial defensive environment look stronger than a complete one by omitting allowed volume; the resulting effect has not been isolated here.

Every opposing attempt was linked to at least one listed defending skater in the reconciliation. This establishes event coverage, not complete or correct attribution of every on-ice player.

The defensive blend lowered team SAT in all six cases. Nevertheless, its inputs use descriptive historical on-ice rates, excluded-event profiles, and provisional division by five across all situations. Large gaps against full on-ice actuals cannot be assigned entirely to defensive talent. Comparing predicted xGA with observed goals is an outcome residual, not a comparison with actual shot-quality xGA.

## Goalies: team-score accuracy can conceal goalie error

| Goalie | Projected GA | Actual GA | Error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Spencer Knight | 3.304 | 3 | +0.304 |
| Sergei Bobrovsky | 3.155 | 2 | +1.155 |
| Arturs Silovs | 3.058 | 0 | +3.058 |
| Igor Shesterkin | 3.158 | 1 | +2.158 |
| Scott Wedgewood | 3.115 | 1 | +2.115 |
| Darcy Kuemper | 3.455 | 4 | -0.545 |

Pittsburgh scored three, but only **one** was charged to Shesterkin; two were empty-net goals. The close team prediction of 3.158 therefore masks a **+2.158 goalie GA error**. This static calculation has no separate empty-net phase and cannot treat team scoring and starter goals allowed as interchangeable evaluation targets.

Across the night, baseline offensive xGF summed to approximately **19.557**. After defensive blending and conversion it was **19.172**; goalie adjustment moved it to **19.245**. Thus the scoring excess was already present before goalie skill. The goalie layer's net adjustment was only **+0.073**, although opposite individual adjustments can cancel in a total.

We have not scored the actual shots with a compatible expected-goals model, so actual GSAx is unavailable. These reports cannot establish whether goalie skill itself, as opposed to the predicted workload and shot quality, was accurate.

## What this night supports investigating next

1. **Align the prediction and evaluation populations.** Explicitly distinguish all SAT from modeled eligible SAT, and separate empty-net scoring from goalie workload.
2. **Reconcile roster opportunity.** Examine team minute budgets, manpower splits, and fallback roles, especially the excess COL/LAK minutes.
3. **Fill defensive opportunity gaps.** Missing skaters must not silently reduce the opponent's composed defensive volume.
4. **Evaluate conversion separately from volume.** Use predicted versus observed attempts, SOG, and goals on matching populations. Test whether personal bucket rates require more support when samples are small.
5. **Measure goalie performance against actual chance quality.** Raw score error cannot identify the quality of the goalie adjustment.
6. **Expand chronological evaluation.** Compare against simple baselines and assess score distributions, win probabilities, and calibration over many games before choosing new weights or claiming improvement.

These are investigation priorities, not approved code changes. Sep2026's listed training seasons exclude opening night, but it was built later and these calculations use known participants. No tuning/provenance audit was performed. The observed strengths are correct winner ordering, some close team estimates, and broad input coverage; the clearest struggles are high scoring/SOG, opportunity allocation, incomplete defensive coverage, and inconsistent attempt populations.
